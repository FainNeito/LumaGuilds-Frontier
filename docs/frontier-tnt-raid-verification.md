# Frontier TNT raid verification

Requirement: test native TNT ignition and chaining, protect vault raid rules, and keep Bloom unchanged while testing.

On 2026-11-13, a regression test demonstrated that TNT attributed to a defending guild member was incorrectly processed as an attacker hit. The objective listener now passes the defender flag to the same siege service used by direct mining. The test failed before the change and passed afterward. The focused raid explosion, siege, and leaderboard suites passed (13 tests); `shadowJar` built the isolated lab artifact. A Byte Buddy startup agent was needed in this Windows environment because dynamic attachment failed.

An isolated Paper 26.4 Snapshot 8 lab ran the candidate plugin and a temporary probe in force-loaded chunks. Real redstone ignition produced one REDSTONE prime event and an explosion with no player source; a spawned TNT explosion primed a second TNT block with cause EXPLOSION, and the resulting TNT exploded. Four explosions were observed in total, including an entity left from the initial unloaded-chunk attempt, so that total is not a clean cannon throughput measurement. The probe was removed from the active lab plugins after shutdown. Evidence: `frontier-26.4-snapshot8/boards-portal-lab/cannon-probe/redstone-chain.log`.

The preceding lab run documented in `frontier-combat-boards.md` tested player-sourced and source-less spawned TNT against two-chunk vault claims, loss of both chunks, one credited victory or no fabricated credit, and persistence after restart. The latest test is not a real player-lit or redstone-cannon attack against a protected vault. Force-loaded lab TNT was source-less; the direct defender fix therefore does not prevent defenders from using unattributed TNT against their own vault. Player-lit chain attribution, cannon trajectory and timing, water defenses, simultaneous blasts, adjacent claim boundaries, cooldowns after real cannon destruction, and adversarial player interaction remain to be tested. No fix from this work has been deployed to Bloom.

Follow-up: the user chose explicit cannon arming. During the raid window `/raidarm` marks existing TNT within eight blocks in each direction for 60 seconds; the first armer's assignment cannot be replaced by another player until it expires or is consumed. Player-sourced direct ignition and explosion chains are passed to spawned TNT; unarmed, unattributed TNT still explodes normally but cannot damage a founding vault. The armer must remain online until the TNT primes. Blocks inserted later, TNT dispensed as an entity, TNT loaded after arming, and TNT on a server restart are not covered by this temporary block registration. TNT claimed by someone else must be re-armed after its existing registration expires. TNT registered by a defender is handled as a defender hit. A source or membership change during a fuse is not stored as a historical guild snapshot.

Focused arming, raid explosion, and siege tests: 17 passed; the lab candidate built. An isolated Snapshot 8 probe confirmed real redstone prime-before-spawn event order, and confirmed a chained explosion prime event contains its parent TNT and occurs before its child spawn. Its redstone and chain entities were source-less, so this is not proof of actual player arming or vault-credit propagation in-game. Evidence is `frontier-26.4-snapshot8/boards-portal-lab/cannon-probe/arming-event-order.log`. A player-armed cannon against a vault, real leaderboard credit, disconnect/restart behavior, and long-range cannon mechanics remain unverified. Bloom remains unchanged.

Player-armed integration follow-up, 2026-11-13: on the isolated Snapshot 8 lab with a signed-in Dawn 26.3 player in LabGuild, `/raidarm` marked TNT that the lab's redstone subsequently ignited. A redstone blast destroyed PowderTest's vault, removed its two partitions and incremented LabGuild's vault score from 2 to 3; the result appeared in the client dialog. A separate armed explosion chain destroyed CannonTarget and incremented the score from 3 to 4. Finally, an armed two-charge launcher powered by lab-triggered timed redstone propelled a primed TNT payload from x=1096.3 to x=1098.7, where it exploded beside LaunchTarget's vault. The entity retained the player source in the lab's movement and explosion logs, the vault became UNAVAILABLE, the target's claim and both partitions were removed, and LabGuild's score incremented exactly once from 4 to 5. The score and claim loss remained after a server restart. For this integration setup only, the raid was open and vault durability was reduced from 20 to 4 so one payload could trigger defeat; the previous multi-blast test used 20. Evidence: `frontier-26.4-snapshot8/boards-portal-lab/cannon-probe/player-redstone-vault.log`, `player-chain-vault.log`, and `player-launched-vault.log`.

The player armed the TNT in-game; a test-only lab probe operated the redstone timing. This establishes a short launched shot, not general cannon reliability: long-range trajectories, varying timings and defenses, player-operated redstone, dispenser-fired TNT, disconnects, and cancellation/lag behavior still require testing. The test found `/raidarm` misleadingly told a player who armed zero blocks to fire; that message now tells them to move within range. Bloom was not modified.

Long-range and player-operated follow-up, 2026-11-13: a signed-in 26.3 player used `/raidarm` and physically clicked a lever in the isolated Snapshot 8 lab. Vanilla redstone dust and repeaters powered the cannon; no probe triggered either charge or payload. Instrumentation recorded player-sourced TNT explosions about 31 and 50 horizontal blocks from the payload position in separate shots. The probe did not yet distinguish the payload from every propelled charge on those two shots, so the 50-block value is the maximum attributed TNT blast, not confirmed payload travel. With payload-specific tracking enabled, a subsequent player-operated shot moved the payload from approximately (1296,99,1300) to (1315,58,1298) before it exploded, retaining the arming player as its source. The distant vault was missed in both target attempts: LongshotTarget remained AVAILABLE, its two claimed partitions survived, and the attacker's score stayed at 5. The cannon exhibited substantial variation between attempts; this limited test does not establish whether the variation is a server defect, cannon design, or ordinary TNT randomness. No production code was changed. The live server and world were not touched; the lab's temporary raid schedule and lowered vault durability were restored after shutdown. Evidence: `cannon-probe/player-longshot-calibration.log`, `player-longshot-first-target.log` and `player-longshot-second-target.log` under `frontier-26.4-snapshot8/boards-portal-lab`.

Controlled long-range follow-up, 2026-11-13: Eight shots of the original lab cannon were recorded, four with native random initial TNT motion and four with horizontal motion zeroed by a test-only probe. Native payload impacts ranged from x=1314.75 to 1343.82; the zeroed group still ranged from x=1311.97 to 1326.60. The sample does not isolate every influence, but zeroing initial motion alone did not make this build repeatable. Logs show only the first five charge blocks primed when redstone was triggered; the second bank was instead primed around four seconds later by explosions. A revised lab wire path primed one additional charge on trigger; the remaining four still primed late, so its wiring is not a complete fix. The added path also changed the trajectory; the result is not proof that any single factor caused the entire spread. An additional repeater trial did not fix the late priming.

For the independent vault attempt, the existing lab target vault was moved to (1346,65,1300), within its two existing claim partitions and at a height observed in the preceding eight-shot calibration. On the second subsequent shot, the payload fired from (1296,99,1300) and exploded at (1340.38,65.08,1300.49), retaining the player source. The explosion defeated the vault, removed the claim and both partitions, and incremented LabGuild's persisted score from 5 to 6. The signed-in 26.3 player was armed through the player command; a lab probe operated the lever and rebuilt the cannon between shots. This is a roughly 44-block horizontal payload flight and is not a fresh physical player lever click. The isolated test temporarily used four objective hits, rather than the usual 20. The target's position was set before this independent firing, not moved during flight. Evidence: cannon-probe/sweep-original.log, sweep-rewired.log, sweep-vault-hit.log, and sweep-repeater.log in the isolated lab. Bloom and its world were unchanged. The lab config and plugin were restored after testing, and the temporary sweep plugin was removed from active plugins. Reliable synchronized firing and live player-operated repeatability still need work; no server physics fix was deployed or claimed.
